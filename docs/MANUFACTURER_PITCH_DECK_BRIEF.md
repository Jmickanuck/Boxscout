# BoxScout Manufacturer Pitch Deck Brief

Last updated: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

This document is the durable handoff for a future dedicated chat/workstream to create a professional manufacturer-facing BoxScout pitch deck / concept PDF, initially aimed at Topps.

The deck should explain:

1. what BoxScout is;
2. what collector problem it solves;
3. how BoxScout would present manufacturer products in the web app;
4. how official manufacturer imagery would be used;
5. why official imagery/data access benefits the manufacturer;
6. the long-term BoxScout vision without overstating current functionality.

This is a communications/design artifact, not an execution plan for the core application.

## Context

BoxScout has contacted Topps regarding use of official product/card imagery. If Topps responds positively, provides sample imagery/assets, or sends a physical sample set/product, BoxScout should be prepared to show a polished explanation of intended usage and strategic value.

The deck should not frame the request as "BoxScout needs your images to function." Instead, the framing should be:

> Official manufacturer assets let BoxScout represent Topps products accurately, professionally, and consistently to collectors making sealed-product purchase decisions.

Do not imply an official Topps affiliation unless one actually exists.

## Core BoxScout positioning

BoxScout is a mobile-first soccer-card sealed-box buying intelligence platform.

Primary user question:

> Which box should I buy, what should I pay, and where should I buy it?

Cards/checklists are supporting intelligence used to explain the contents, odds, chase quality and remaining opportunity of a sealed product.

## Recommended deck length

Target approximately 6–8 pages. It should be highly visual and understandable in roughly two minutes.

## Recommended deck structure

### 1. Cover

- BoxScout logo/name
- short positioning line, e.g. "Helping collectors choose the right sealed product"
- boxscout.io
- professional mockup of the future BoxScout interface

### 2. The collector problem

Explain the fragmented buying experience:

- many sealed formats/configurations are hard to distinguish;
- prices vary across retailers;
- pull odds and exclusives are difficult to compare;
- checklists and chase information are fragmented;
- older sealed products become harder to evaluate as major finite hits surface.

### 3. What BoxScout does

Show the decision flow:

Release / Set -> Box Format -> Exact Configuration -> Price / Where to Buy -> Contents / Odds -> Eligible Chases -> Surfaced Evidence -> Compare

Explain that BoxScout aims to consolidate trusted purchase intelligence in one place.

### 4. Example web-app experience

This is a critical page. Use realistic diagrams/mockups rather than only prose.

The first deck should show four core screens:

#### A. Homepage / intelligence dashboard

Show examples of:
- Featured Boxes
- new/notable releases
- latest major verified pull
- latest major card sale
- sealed price movement / price drops
- recent BoxScout updates

The mature homepage should feel alive and decision-oriented rather than like a static marketing page.

#### B. Release / set page

Show a Topps release leading to the sealed ways a collector can buy/open it:
- Hobby
- Mega
- Blaster
- other relevant formats

Each box-format card can illustrate future fields such as:
- typical/average price
- lowest credible offer
- pack/card counts
- important guarantees/averages
- number of known configurations
- notable exclusives
- headline chase information

#### C. Specific box/configuration page

This is the core purchase-decision mockup.

Show, where relevant:
- exact box identity
- typical/current sealed price
- cheapest credible retailer offer
- where to buy
- packs/cards
- published odds or source-qualified hit averages/guarantees
- top eligible chases
- surfaced finite-card status
- drill-down to exact eligible checklist

Do not show invented real-world numbers as facts. Clearly use illustrative/demo data unless sourced.

#### D. Visual card checklist / gallery

Show how official Topps imagery could appear:
- mobile-first visual gallery
- card image
- card number/player
- subset/parallel filtering
- exact box eligibility filtering
- deeper chase/variant detail

This screen should make clear why official imagery materially improves collector understanding and product presentation.

### 5. How official imagery would be used

Explain that manufacturer assets would support:

- accurate card identification;
- visual checklist/gallery browsing;
- chase-card presentation;
- sealed-product pages;
- box comparison;
- editorial/product intelligence;
- clear manufacturer attribution.

BoxScout should not imply ownership of Topps IP or imagery. Usage should follow the scope of whatever permission/license Topps provides.

Image identity and publication rights remain separate concepts in BoxScout's data model.

### 6. Why this can benefit Topps

Potential manufacturer benefits to communicate carefully:

- makes Topps sealed products easier for collectors to understand;
- helps collectors distinguish formats/configurations and exclusives;
- showcases premium and chase cards professionally;
- can direct qualified collectors toward retailers selling Topps products;
- keeps older sealed releases discoverable after launch;
- gives collectors an additional discovery/research surface before purchase;
- reduces confusion caused by fragmented third-party information.

Useful framing:

> Topps creates the product. BoxScout helps collectors understand what makes each sealed product interesting and where they can buy it.

Do not claim BoxScout will increase Topps sales unless evidence exists; present this as the intended value proposition.

### 7. Long-term vision

Show a polished future-state dashboard with concepts such as:
- Featured Boxes
- new Topps releases
- major verified pulls
- major card sales
- sealed price history
- chase landscape
- compare boxes

Make clear which capabilities are future direction rather than current live functionality.

### 8. About / contact

- independent BoxScout project
- boxscout.io
- contact@boxscout.io
- no claim of official manufacturer affiliation unless formally established

## Visual direction

The PDF should look like the BoxScout product, not a generic corporate slide template.

Preferred characteristics:

- dark BoxScout visual identity with lime/green/cream accents where appropriate;
- large clean mockups;
- minimal text per page;
- diagrams and annotated UI screens;
- mobile-first product screenshots/mockups displayed in phone/browser frames where useful;
- factual and professional rather than hype-heavy.

The web-app diagrams/mockups matter more than decorative graphics.

## Mockup rules

- Mockups may illustrate future features that are already approved product direction.
- Clearly avoid presenting illustrative/demo prices, sales, odds, chase counts or surfaced counts as verified live facts.
- Use labels such as "Concept", "Illustrative", or "Future BoxScout experience" where necessary.
- Do not fabricate Topps logos/assets/card imagery if usage permission has not been obtained; placeholders or clearly marked conceptual imagery can be used until rights are confirmed.
- Once Topps provides approved assets, replace placeholders only within the permission scope provided.

## If Topps sends a physical sample set/product

Before using it in the deck or public app:

- preserve the email/letter/terms that accompanied it;
- document packaging and exact product identifiers before opening;
- record date received and what was supplied;
- distinguish permission to possess samples from permission to reproduce/publish images;
- keep high-resolution source photography/scans private unless publication is permitted;
- map images deterministically to exact card/checklist/variant identities;
- create web derivatives only after rights review;
- retain provenance for every public asset.

A manufacturer-provided sample can become the benchmark workflow for BoxScout's future manufacturer asset pipeline.

## Deliverables for the dedicated pitch-deck chat

The dedicated chat should ultimately produce:

1. final deck narrative and page order;
2. exact copy for each page;
3. four or more polished BoxScout web-app mockup screens;
4. diagrams showing how manufacturer imagery/data flows into the collector experience;
5. final professional PDF;
6. optionally a shorter email attachment/version if Topps requests something concise.

Justin should not need design/software expertise to produce this. His role is product owner/reviewer; ChatGPT should handle structure, copy, mockup direction, visual creation and PDF assembly while asking only questions that materially affect the intended manufacturer message.
